1. Product Requirements Document — StockFlow

1.1 Product Overview

Product Name: StockFlow
Product Type: Inventory Management SaaS

Target Customers:

  • Small and medium businesses
  • Retail stores
  • Wholesalers
  • Distributors
  • E-commerce businesses
  • Businesses operating multiple warehouses

Product Vision

StockFlow helps businesses manage their inventory, purchases, sales, suppliers, and warehouses from one centralized platform.
The goal is to replace spreadsheets and disconnected systems with a single source of truth.

2. The Problem

Many businesses manage inventory using:

Excel + Paper invoices + WhatsApp + Manual calculations + Multiple employees

This creates problems:

  • They don't know the exact stock level.
  • Products run out unexpectedly.
  • Overstocking wastes money.
  • Employees make data-entry mistakes.
  • Supplier information is scattered.
  • Managers don't have real-time reports.
  • Multiple warehouses are difficult to manage.

3. The Solution

4. Users & Roles

This is important because it will eventually affect your authentication and authorization design.

Owner

Full access.

Owner
 ├── Manage business
 ├── Manage employees
 ├── Manage warehouses
 ├── Manage products
 ├── View reports
 └── Manage subscription

Admin

Almost everything except critical account/billing operations.

Manager

  • Products
  • Inventory
  • Purchases
  • Sales
  • Reports

Employee

Limited operational access. For example:

  • View Products
  • Create Sales
  • Update Inventory

but cannot:

  • Delete Business
  • Manage Users
  • Change Subscription

12. Alerts

StockFlow should automatically detect:

Stock <= MinimumStock

and generate:

⚠️ Wireless Mouse is running low — only 5 units remaining.

Other possible alerts:

  • Low stock
  • Out of stock
  • Purchase order received
  • Failed payment
  • Unusual inventory adjustment

This eventually gives you an opportunity to use background jobs.

15. Subscription Model

Because we're designing this as a SaaS, you can eventually have:

Starter

  • 1 Warehouse
  • 1,000 Products
  • 3 Users
  • Basic Reports

Business

  • 5 Warehouses
  • 10,000 Products
  • 20 Users
  • Advanced Reports

Enterprise

  • Unlimited Warehouses
  • Unlimited Products
  • Unlimited Users
  • Advanced Analytics
  • Priority Support

You don't need to implement payments now. But designing the product this way means you can add something like Stripe/Paymob later.

21. One thing I'd change from the generated mockup

The generated design is intentionally quite dense because it's showing a concept. For your implementation, don't make everything visible at once. Use whitespace.

Your hierarchy should be:

Hero → Product visual → Features → Proof → How it works → Pricing → CTA

And make the dashboard mockup the main visual asset. That immediately communicates:

"This isn't just a website. This company actually has a software product."
